This project looks at how well machine learning models can predict NFL game
outcomes using historical team data. Data is collected from Pro Football Reference
(https://www.pro-football-reference.com) by exporting season-level team stats from the
past ten years into CSV files and loading them into Pandas Data Frames. The dataset
includes features such as points scored and allowed, total yards, turnover differential, and
other overall team performance metrics.


Because the data is at the team level rather than individual games, a game-level
dataset is created by matching teams against each other and comparing their statistics
(for example, differences in offensive production, defensive performance, and efficiency
metrics). Additional feature engineering is applied to better represent team strength and
relative advantages between opponents.


Two models are developed and evaluated: logistic regression and a random forest
classifier. Logistic regression serves as a simple and interpretable baseline, while the
random forest model is used to capture more complex and nonlinear relationships in the
data. Model performance is evaluated under different conditions, including varying feature
sets and training data sizes.


The goal of this project is to determine how effectively team-level statistics can be
used to predict game outcomes and to identify which features are most useful in making
those predictions, while also exploring the strengths and limitations of different modeling
approaches.
